Current Research Direction

From Detecting Misinformation to Observing Change

My Master's research began with a seemingly simple question: what makes misinformation different from other information? Following that question gradually led me from definitions, diffusion, cognition, platforms, and detection toward a different problem — how we can continue to observe a socio-technical system when the system itself does not remain still.

Follow the Research Question ↓
01 · Where the Question Began

What actually makes misinformation different?

My Master's research initially approached misinformation as a conceptual problem. Calling something "misinformation" appears straightforward only if truth, intention, evidence, and context are themselves straightforward. In practice, they are not.

If misinformation and true information are defined differently, should that difference also become visible in the way they behave inside a communication system?

QUESTION 01

Definition

Misinformation cannot be understood only as another class label. Questions of truth, misleading content, intention, evidence, and observable intent shape what exactly we mean when we call information "misinformation."

QUESTION 02

Observable Behaviour

If misinformation differs conceptually, can that difference be observed through diffusion patterns, cascades, network structures, interaction behaviour, or patterns of social reinforcement?

QUESTION 03

Mechanism

Observable diffusion is not itself an explanation. The next question becomes why these patterns emerge and what mechanisms continuously produce and reshape them.

02 · Why Does It Behave Differently?

The answer is neither purely human nor purely technological.

Misinformation circulates within socio-technical environments. Human interpretation and platform mechanisms do not operate independently: they repeatedly shape one another.

Human Interpretation

People do not receive information as passive endpoints. Existing beliefs, attention, emotion, social identity, trust, and reinforcement from others influence how information is interpreted and whether it is accepted, repeated, or challenged.

Cognition Attention Belief Social Reinforcement Interpretation

Platform Dynamics

At the same time, modern communication platforms mediate what becomes visible. Ranking, recommendation, engagement feedback, community structures, and algorithmic amplification reshape the information environment in which human interpretation occurs.

03 · How Do We Observe It Now?

Detection methods observe different fragments of the same system.

Existing computational approaches have become increasingly sophisticated. Yet each family of methods tends to observe a particular representation of misinformation rather than the entire adaptive environment in which it exists.

NLP

Content-based approaches examine linguistic and semantic characteristics of claims, narratives, and documents.

Interaction

Behavioural methods use how users respond, share, comment, engage, or coordinate around information.

Networks

Graph-based methods capture relationships among users, communities, sources, cascades, and diffusion structures.

Evidence

Evidence-based methods connect claims with external information, reference records, or fact-verification resources.

The Observation Gap

But what if the thing we are trying to detect does not remain the same?

Claims can be reformulated. Evidence can evolve. Platform environments can change. User behaviour can shift. Models can drift. And sometimes the state of the world itself changes. A detector trained under one configuration of these relationships may therefore continue producing outputs even after the meaning of those outputs has begun to change.

04 · What Exactly Became Unstable?

A changed prediction does not tell us what changed.

This ambiguity became the point where my Master's research began to evolve into my current research proposal. When observation becomes unreliable, several very different explanations may be possible.

The Model Changed

The statistical relationship learned by the model may no longer describe the incoming data.

The Evidence Changed

A reference record that was once appropriate may have become incomplete, outdated, or superseded by new evidence.

The Claim Changed

Meaning-preserving reformulation or contextual change may alter model behaviour even when the substantive claim remains similar.

The World Changed

Sometimes the underlying state of reality evolves, making yesterday's reference point inappropriate for today's observation.

05 · The Shift

From detection as an endpoint to warning as a beginning.

My proposed direction is therefore not simply to build another classifier. I want to shift the centre of attention from producing increasingly confident detection decisions toward identifying when the relationship between a model and its observation environment may require reconsideration.

Detection-Centred

What is the label?

Observe an information object, process it through a model, and produce a classification or verification outcome.

→
Warning-Centred

Should we review the system?

Observe indicators of instability and trigger interpretive review when the relationship among model, evidence, claim, and environment may no longer be reliable.

06 · How Might This Work?

Observation becomes a recursive process rather than a one-off decision.

The framework I am exploring uses early-warning signals to decide when human interpretation becomes valuable, rather than requiring continuous manual review of every observation.

1

Continuous Observation

The system continuously observes claims, model behaviour, evidence relationships, and other available indicators.

2

Early-Warning Signal

A signal is generated when observed behaviour suggests that the current configuration may no longer be stable.

3

Triggered Human Review

Instead of automatically assuming model failure, selected cases are escalated for contextual interpretation.

4

Diagnose the Source of Instability

Human judgement helps determine whether the problem lies in the model, evidence record, claim context, changing world state, or their interaction.

5

Targeted Adaptation

The model, reference record, both, or neither may then be updated according to the diagnosis.

6

Continue Observing

The revised system returns to observation, allowing the cycle to repeat as the environment continues to evolve.

07 · Human as Agent

The human is not simply an annotator at the end of the pipeline.

In this framework, human judgement acts as an interpretive anchor. Its role is not merely to supply another ground-truth label, but to reconnect computational observation with context when the system can no longer determine by itself what has changed.

The Model

Detects patterns, monitors signals, and identifies conditions under which the current observational relationship may have become unstable.

Interpretive Anchor

HUMAN

Interprets why the warning occurred, situates it in context, and determines what — if anything — should be revised.

The World

Continues changing independently of the assumptions under which the computational model was originally constructed.

08 · Why This Matters

The goal is not to build a model that asks reality to remain compatible with its assumptions. The goal is to notice when that compatibility begins to break.

I hope this direction can help move misinformation detection away from treating computational models as closed decision systems. Instead, the model becomes one component of a recursive observation process in which human agency can repeatedly reconnect computational representations with the changing reality they are intended to describe.
